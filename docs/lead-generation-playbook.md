# Lead Generation Playbook

A practical, privacy-aware workflow for turning a clear audience definition into qualified marketing conversations.

## 1. Define the Ideal Customer Profile

Write down the assumptions before collecting leads:

- **Industry:** Which market has the problem you solve?
- **Company size:** What revenue, headcount, or operating scale fits?
- **Buyer role:** Who owns the problem and the budget?
- **Trigger:** What event makes the problem urgent?
- **Current alternative:** How do they solve it today?
- **Disqualifiers:** Which prospects should not enter the pipeline?

A strong ICP is specific enough to guide research and broad enough to produce a useful market.

## 2. Build a Lead Research Brief

For each campaign, record:

- Campaign goal and success metric
- Target segment and geography
- Approved data sources
- Required evidence for qualification
- Personalization points worth researching
- Owner, review date, and retention period

Prefer first-party sources, public business information, and permission-based lists. Do not collect sensitive personal data or scrape sites that prohibit automated access.

## 3. Qualify Leads

Use a simple score rather than intuition alone:

| Signal | Example | Score |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| ICP fit | Target industry and size | 0-3 |
| Problem fit | Clear evidence of the relevant pain | 0-3 |
| Timing | Recent trigger or active initiative | 0-2 |
| Access | A relevant public contact route | 0-2 |
| Intent | Requested information or replied | 0-3 |

Define a threshold before outreach. A high score means **prioritize research**, not permission to send unlimited messages.

## 4. Create Useful Outreach

A good first message should be:

1. Relevant to the recipient's role or situation.
2. Specific about the problem you observed.
3. Honest about why you are contacting them.
4. Small in its request: ask for a reply, referral, or short conversation.
5. Easy to decline.

Example structure:

> Hi [name], I noticed [specific business signal]. Teams in [segment] often run into [problem]. We are testing [relevant approach]. Would a short exchange about how you handle this be useful? No problem if it is not a priority.

Never imply a relationship, result, or endorsement that does not exist.

## 5. Follow-Up Sequence

Keep follow-ups limited and useful:

- **Day 0:** Initial context and small question
- **Day 4:** Add one relevant insight or resource
- **Day 10:** Ask whether the topic belongs with someone else
- **Day 17:** Close the loop and stop contacting them

Stop immediately when someone opts out, asks not to be contacted, or indicates the message is irrelevant.

## 6. CRM Fields

Track the minimum information needed to operate the campaign:

- Company and website
- Segment and ICP score
- Source and source date
- Contact role, if publicly provided or consented
- Problem hypothesis and evidence URL
- Last touch, next action, and owner
- Consent or lawful-basis note
- Opt-out status

Keep an audit trail for edits and delete records when the retention period ends.

## 7. Measure the Funnel

Review weekly:

- Qualified leads created
- Positive reply rate
- Meeting or discovery rate
- Conversion by source and segment
- Time from first touch to qualified conversation
- Opt-out, complaint, and bounce rates
- Cost per qualified opportunity

Optimize for qualified conversations and customer value, not message volume.

## 8. Responsible Automation

Automation can help with deduplication, reminders, enrichment from approved sources, and reporting. Keep human review for qualification, personalization, sending, and deletion decisions.

Before launch, verify:

- The source permits the intended use.
- Outreach follows applicable privacy and marketing rules.
- Every message has a clear sender and opt-out path.
- Rate limits and retries are conservative.
- Personal data is protected and not committed to source control.
- The campaign can be stopped quickly.

## Campaign Checklist

- [ ] ICP and disqualifiers documented
- [ ] Data sources reviewed and approved
- [ ] Qualification threshold defined
- [ ] Outreach reviewed for relevance and honesty
- [ ] Opt-out handling tested
- [ ] CRM fields and retention period set
- [ ] Baseline metrics recorded
- [ ] Stop conditions agreed

A sustainable lead-generation system earns attention by being relevant, transparent, and useful.
